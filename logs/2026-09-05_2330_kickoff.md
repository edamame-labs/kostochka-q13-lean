## Results (added 2026-09-05 late session)
- Exhaustive search, no counterexample: k=3 n≤8 (p=5); k=4 n≤8 (p=5, p=6 for n=7); k=5 n≤9 (p=6).
  Commands: `python3 search.py n k p --first` (outputs in scripts/out/).
- Literature: Kaul–Mudrock–Wagstrom arXiv:2008.06333 Prop. 22 gives a two-star forest that
  is not equitably k-choosable (KPW). Checked: it satisfies the α_v condition. So Q13 is FALSE.
- Sharpened to n = 2k³ + k: K_{1,(k−1)(2k²+1)−1} + K_{1,2k²}, two leaves per (c,d) pair with
  list ([k]−c) ∪ {d}, slack 1. Tree variant by adding one leaf–leaf edge. Full proof in
  notes/COUNTEREXAMPLE.md.
- Verified by `python3 verify_counterexample.py` for k=3,4 (F, tree, KMW): α_v OK,
  explicit equitable colouring OK, no SE L-colouring (exact flow-based check).
- Positive controls on the verifier: see log below.

## Dead ends / what failed and why
- Tried to shrink the example to slack 0 (one stuck leaf per pair): then |B| = q − 2 and
  α_{u₀} = q − 1 fails (C) by exactly one — this is Theorem 27's obstruction in disguise.
- Degree-2 "stuck" vertices adjacent to both centres would cut the count to 2k, but two such
  vertices create a 4-cycle; only one is allowed in a forest.
- Letting L(w₀) meet [k] gives the colourer an escape (choose d ≠ c), so L(w₀) ∩ [k] = ∅ is needed.
